# Ground truth anchors

An evaluation that only checks internal consistency cannot find the most
important defects. For each claim, reach the original problem statement and the
cited literature, then ask whether the invariant the atlas names is the invariant
the mathematics actually has.

## How to use this

1. Open the erdosproblems.com anchor for the problem id.
2. Read the *original* statement. Note precisely what it quantifies over.
3. Compare against `math_invariant` in the claim. These should be the same object.
4. For each citation in `citations_as_given`, check whether it is about this
   correspondence or merely adjacent. Record which.
5. Then, and only then, judge the physics side.

## What is missing here

For most problems the anchor is the erdosproblems.com statement alone. That is a
source for the *problem*, not for the morphism. There is no external authority
that has blessed any of these correspondences, and the atlas has not been peer
reviewed. Your independent judgement is the check.

If a citation cannot be located, record it as a gap rather than guessing.
Claims whose only support is a citation you could not find are weaker than their
`strength` field suggests, and saying so is a finding.
